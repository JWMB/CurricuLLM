# Discussion Synthesizer
This folder is intended as an area for synthesising knowledge about controversial topics, primarily related to learning, EdTech and Big Tech.

Each topic 
* has its own folder (where sub-topic folders may later appear)
* has some required sections, e.g. **Terminology**, that adds on to the sections of the parent topics 

Each proposition must be decorated with a link to a section that contains explanations and in-depth sources (preferrably to internet archive sites).

While experimenting with the format, all topics are located in this file.

## Terminology
Terms that might be contentious, or that might be used slightly differently from common usage, are defined here for clarity
| Term      | Definition |
| ----------- | ----------- |
| Big Tech | Shorthand for US-owned tech companies |
| Data sovereignty | Complete national or territorial control over data, <br/> no authorized access from foreign actors |

## Topic: The EU should avoid Big Tech
### Propositions
#### Data integrity
* The CLOUD Act allows - in contradiction with GDPR - for US-owned companies to hand over any data that the US government requests [^](#cloud-act)
* The US government has demonstrated its disdain for the rule of law, both domestically and internationally 
	* Acts against court orders [^](#us-administration-disobeys-us-courts)
	* Allows ICE to act outside of the country's laws
	* Capture of foreign leader 
	* Threats to capture Greenland
	* Threats to capture Canada
* The US government has been involved in efforts to bolster right-wing groups in the EU and Canada
* The US government sees tech services as a way to meddle with public opinion, domestically and internationally
	* TikTok and X
* The US government has threatened tariffs when proposals to switch from US services has been made
 
#### Resilience
* The US government has demonstrated its willingness to shut off access to US-controlled services for unwanted elements
	* ICC
	* Bill Gates interview

#### Outsourcing of competence
* System administrators have gone from deep technological knowledge to being "click-ops"

#### Capital flight
* Some 1.5% of EU GDP goes to the US for ICT; money that could be spent on building EU infrastructure and competence.
